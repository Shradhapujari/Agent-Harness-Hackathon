# Hush Design Direction

<!-- impeccable:design-schema 1 -->

## World

Hush is an incident observatory: a calm, daylight-readable operations surface inspired by a seismic station. Alarm noise appears as a trace, investigation converges on an epicenter, and the graph reads as a relay of accountable work. The metaphor is structural, not decorative.

## Composition

The incident is the dominant field. A narrow fixed-column relay shows graph progression beside it; evidence and actions form registers below. Service health and run identity occupy one compact horizontal ribbon. Avoid grids of interchangeable summary cards.

## Typography

Use Newsreader for the product-scale title and resolved finding, and Commissioner for controls and operational prose. Use the platform monospace only for identifiers, node codes, and tool names. Numerals that update in place are tabular.

## Color and Material

Warm mineral paper is the ground, with dark green-black ink. Vermilion is reserved for active signal and destructive boundaries; mineral green marks healthy or completed states. Fine rules and plotted lines create depth without decorative glass or shadows.

## Interaction

One authored motion carries the product story: an incident trace draws and a scanning cursor begins when a run starts. The human checkpoint rises from the bottom because it changes authority, not merely attention. All motion collapses under reduced-motion preferences.

## Controls and States

Controls use explicit verbs. Service connectivity, graph progress, evidence provenance, action kind, decision state, errors, disabled states, and empty states remain legible without relying on color alone. Denial requires a reason; approval applies only to the exact pending action and run.

## Responsive Behavior

On narrower screens the agent relay follows the signal field, registers stack, and the approval checkpoint becomes a scrollable single-column sheet. The signal remains the visual anchor; no mobile-only information is removed.
